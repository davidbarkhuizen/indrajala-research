\begin{abstract}
Our method learns a finite-state transducer from input--output examples and certifies the learned
machine exactly. Its main failure had been a state that never forms, or forms and is lost when
discrete codes take it over. We separate the two (\emph{formation} and \emph{conversion}) with one
measurement, and find that in a snapped recurrent state formation turns on the target's algebra: reset-like machines
(move-to-front) form their state from the whole length distribution at once, while groups (the word
problem of $S_3$), having no synchronising word, need it \emph{seeded} on short words first.
Conversion succeeds as a crystallisation of the codes (heat, melt, slow cool) with a gradual
handover from the continuous state to the codes. With three fixes, each aimed at a failure visible
early in a run's own logs, a population of eight independent $S_3$ runs certified 8 of 8 (Wilson
95\% interval 0.68--1.00), against 2 of 8 without them; move-to-front on 4 items certified under
all three cooling shapes tried. No commitment term, traces or given decomposition were used.
Read exactly, the certified networks' factors are the target's own approximations, and in two
cases a cover of it. The codes' measured heat capacity peaks over each cascade of splits, as
deterministic annealing predicts; a cool paced by it is faster but, read in flight, loses some
formed states where its steps outrun the handover. We set out what this establishes, what it doesn't, and the direction agreed
from it: test the method on the full panel of backdrops, diagnose the failures' common mechanism,
and only then choose a remedy.
\end{abstract}

\section{Setting}

The network (\texttt{snapped-window-recurrence.tex}) is window-recurrent: a small causal transformer
reads a window of input tokens and the current state, and the state is \emph{factored} into $G$
parts, each part's next value snapped to the nearest of its learned codes. A trained network is
therefore a finite-state transducer, and a \emph{certificate} checks it against the target machine
from every reachable state on every input: a proof, not a sample. Every cell is feasible (the codes
can represent the target's minimal states; training reaches the exclusion bound $m + q - 1$), and
every claim below was registered before its data.

Before 20 October, training certified some backdrops, but two failures resisted every remedy:
\emph{failure A}, a state that never forms, and \emph{harm}, the commitment term (which pulls each
cell's output to its code) hurting formation early though some backdrops need it late. The literature
review proposed four options; this note is the first, annealing the snap, rebuilt as a
crystallisation. Its mechanisms follow the literature: soft assignments hold every code at the data's
mean above a critical temperature \citep{rose-1998-deterministic-annealing-clustering}, and quantising
before a direction is learned freezes it \citep{zhao-2026-continuous-first-discrete-later-vq-vae}.

\section{Findings}

\paragraph{Formation and conversion are two problems.} \emph{Formation}: the continuous network's
cell outputs come to carry the target's state. \emph{Conversion}: discrete codes take that state over
without losing it. A \emph{state-separation} measurement (nearest-neighbour and centroid accuracy at
naming the target's state from the cell outputs, the target read only to measure) tells them apart.
An abrupt snap destroyed a formed state: on $S_4$ the joint separation at length 16 fell from 0.98 to
0.25. Earlier remedies aimed at one problem had been judged on runs failing at the other.

\paragraph{In a snapped state, formation depends on the machine's algebra.} Move-to-front, every token a reset of part
of the order, formed its state from the whole length distribution at once: separation 1.00 at
lengths 4--128 at the warm-up's end. The word problem of $S_3$, every token a permutation, did not
(centroid accuracy at chance from length 16). With a curriculum its continuous state formed (1.00 to
length 32), but snapped onto its own k-means codes it fell to chance (0.41 at 16, 0.20 at 64): a
group has no synchronising word, so one wrong snap stays wrong to the word's end. A group's state is
therefore \emph{seeded}: crystallised in a \emph{crucible} (the curriculum's warm-up, then the anneal
on its last lengths held fixed) before the whole distribution. In Krohn and Rhodes's terms
\citep{krohn-rhodes-1965-algebraic-theory-machines}, a machine with no \emph{prime loop} (no input
word cycling states in a loop of prime length) is built in series and parallel from units
(flip-flops) and delays alone (their Corollary~4.1, I); move-to-front is such a machine (every input word
acts idempotently: repeating it changes nothing), while $S_3$'s word problem has loops of lengths 2 and 3 and needs counters
modulo 2 and 3 (III). The network forms the first kind from the whole distribution and the second only
from a seed.

The claim is this architecture's, not one about gradient descent on groups. A related split is
known as expressivity: state-space models track flip-flops at every length but struggle with parity
\citep{sarrof-2024-expressive-capacity-ssm-formal-language}, and fixed-depth transformers and
state-space models can't track non-solvable groups at every length
(unless $\mathsf{TC}^0 = \mathsf{NC}^1$)
\citep{liu-2022-transformers-shortcuts-automata,merrill-2024-illusion-of-state-ssm} ($S_3$ is
solvable, so this bound doesn't reach it), while single-layer RNNs learn group word problems at
every length \citep{merrill-2024-illusion-of-state-ssm}; and under reinforcement learning, permutation automata
mostly fail a state probe \citep{allchin-2026-reward-teach-state-hidden-automaton}. Seeding on short
strings is \citeauthor{elman-1993-learning-development-starting-small}'s ``starting small''
\citep{elman-1993-learning-development-starting-small}, disputed by
\citet{rohde-1999-language-acquisition-starting-small}. Our mechanism is the snap's: with no
synchronising word, one wrong snap persists. Whether an unsnapped recurrent network of the same size
needs the seed is not yet tested.

\paragraph{Conversion succeeds as a crystallisation with a gradual handover.} The codes are heated
above their measured melting point, held, and cooled slowly. Each factor's next state is
$(1 - \ell)\,h + \ell\,q$ (the cell's output $h$, its code $q$), the coupling $\ell$ rising from 0 to
1 as the temperature falls from the factor's melting point to its last measured transition, so the
codes anneal on the continuous state while the forward path keeps its information. The melt melts
the codes only: on move-to-front the continuous state's separation stayed 1.00 at every length
through it.

\paragraph{A population, and three systematic failures fixed.} Each run is an independent draw, and
the certificate admits no false positives, so the measure is the per-run success rate $p$. The first
population (word-problem-$S_3$ seeds 0--7, each its own crucible) certified seeds 0 and 5. Read in
flight, the six failures were of three kinds, each visible early in the run's own measurements, none
from the target:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{a crucible too short}: three warm-ups ended at length 3 and their best state share stayed
  0.36--0.38;
  \item \emph{an open-loop cool}: the schedule's clock counted epochs, so three runs cooled on through
  a dip in the answers' screen to full coupling and lost a formed state (best shares 0.75--0.93);
  \item \emph{a degenerate coupling window}: one factor melted in a single transition, so its handover
  happened within an octave.
\end{itemize}
Three fixes, one each: the crucible runs to the warm-up's cap (length 8); the cool is
\emph{quasi-static}, its clock holding (temperature and coupling fixed, the noise on) whenever the
screen falls below 0.99 of the hold's best, as adaptive schedules lower the temperature only once
an equilibrium test passes \citep{huang-1986-efficient-cooling-schedule,romeo-1989-simulated-annealing-theory-layout};
and each coupling window spans at least three octaves. A schedule closed on training's own progress
is not new: \citet{das-1993-unified-gradient-clustering-fsm-induction} anneal a soft clustering of a
recurrent network's state to hard with the temperature tied to the training error, the closest
precedent, and \citet{agustsson-2017-soft-to-hard-vector-quantization} tie a soft-to-hard schedule
to the gap between the soft and hard losses. Ours are the coupling window measured per factor and
the clock held on a dip in the answers' screen.

Every registered claim held. Every crucible reached length 8 and formed the state (snapped joint
separation 1.00 at lengths 16 and 64, chance 0.20 and 0.17). Two runs dipped in the cool, and the
clock held through each dip (once to a screen of 0.63) and recovered within 1--3 epochs; no run lost
a formed state or stalled. One run certified while still heating, on the length-8 crucible alone.
On move-to-front with 4 items (24 states), seed~3 certified under all three cooling shapes: convex
at step 104 (168 minutes), s-shaped at 201, concave at 289 (580 minutes). Every certificate is of
exactly the target's states, with the commitment term never on.

\paragraph{The factors are the target's approximations.} A tool reads exactly what each factor's
code determines of the target: the finest homomorphic image (a congruence's quotient) it fixes
\citep{egri-nagy-2015-computational-holonomy-decomposition}, and whether the factors together cover
the target.
\begin{itemize}
  \item $S_3$ seed 7 is a \emph{cover}: its factors are exactly the congruences fixing the place of
  item 1 and of item 2, which together determine the permutation. Seeds 0, 2 and 6 hold one such
  place in one factor; seeds 1, 3, 4 and 5 hold the state only jointly.
  \item Move-to-front's concave certificate is a cover: one factor holds where item 3 stands against
  each of the others, the other the order of items 0--2 (a three-item LRU). Its convex certificate
  holds five of the six pairwise recency orders exactly, the sixth jointly; thirteen earlier
  certified networks hold the same kind of structure.
\end{itemize}
Nothing told the network about these decompositions; it was trained on inputs and answers only. On
move-to-front they are of Krohn and Rhodes's kind: each pairwise recency order is a flip-flop. On
$S_3$ they are not: its prime decomposition is a cascade of the sign (modulo 2) into a counter
modulo 3, but no certified network holds the sign (\texttt{parity}), though transformers trained on
permutation composition often compute it first \citep{li-2025-how-language-models-track-state}. Read as cascades (a factor's
approximation on top, the other factor's codes below it, reading it), seeds 0, 2 and 6 put the place
of an item on top, the other two items' order below: $S_3$'s chain through a point's stabiliser, not
through the sign. Seed 7's cover is a parallel product of two actions on three points, each containing
both loops; seeds 1, 3, 4 and 5 hold the state only jointly. Whether a learned component is
irreducible (its own monoid's groups and loops) is not yet read.

\paragraph{The heat capacity marks the cascades.} In deterministic annealing the soft assignment's
distortion is an energy, and its variance at a temperature a heat capacity, which peaks at the phase
transitions \citep{rose-1998-deterministic-annealing-clustering}. Logged an epoch at a time on three
certified runs (two $S_3$, one move-to-front), with every run certifying at its original step (the
logging is passive), each factor's capacity sits at 0.01--0.06 through the melt, jumps 1.2--2.6 times
as the first codes part, stays high across the cascade of splits (peaks 0.6--1.0) and falls to about
0.001 below the last. It is a hump over the cascade, not a spike at one split (the largest capacity
within two epochs of the largest split in 2 of 6 factors), and noisy from epoch to epoch. The convex
cool's own speed through the cascades, $-\,\mathrm{d}\ln T\,\sqrt{C}$ an epoch, is 0.027--0.034 on
$S_3$ and 0.029--0.037 on move-to-front.

\paragraph{The splits come early.} Training reaches its fine counts of code groups 2--6 octaves
hotter than the melting curve measured at the warm-up's end predicts (one move-to-front factor: 15
groups at $T = 1.19$, predicted at 0.029). The grouping threshold biases the other way. With weak
thermal noise the curve predicted training's splits within a fifth of an octave, so the material
moving after the warm-up, or the noise parting codes, are the candidates; neither is yet read.

\paragraph{Adaptive cooling, in test.} The quasi-static cool follows a precomputed curve and only
holds it through dips. Adaptive schedules \citep{huang-1986-efficient-cooling-schedule,romeo-1989-simulated-annealing-theory-layout}
compute it: the temperature steps once an equilibrium test passes, by $T \exp(-\lambda/\sqrt{C})$, so
the cool slows by itself where the capacity peaks. As built here, each factor's test reads its energy
an epoch at a time (within 0.75 of its spread $T\sqrt{C}$ of the predicted or the previous epoch's),
with $\lambda = 0.03$ (the convex cool's speed above), a step at most an octave, and at most 10 epochs
at a temperature. A one-run smoke test certified $S_3$ seed 0 at step 71 (convex: 85), its steps
slowing to 0.035 in $\ln T$ an epoch at the capacity's peak against 0.10--0.14 in the melt. Its
existence probe (Section~\ref{sec:open}) is running.

\section{Bearing on the direction}

\paragraph{Reliability (stage A).} The project's aim is machine discovery: unknown finite-state
machines found from behaviour and known structure, and proved. Its first stage is one general
architecture and method that forms a state, keeps it and certifies on every backdrop. The
quasi-static crucible is the first candidate that does so on both a group and a reset backdrop
without the commitment term. The evidence is narrow: two backdrops, one cell each, machines of 6 and
24 states. And the three fixes were designed from the very seeds they were then rerun on, so 8 of 8
measures the fixes on their own failures; fresh seeds are the clean measure of $p$.

\paragraph{Discovery.} That certified factors come out as the target's approximations, and sometimes
a cover, without being given, is the decomposition side of discovery: the stage at which known
approximations are supplied or withheld and recovery is measured. It is also the publication's
frame, learning finite-state transducers with proof, strengthened: the method yields a proved
machine and often a proved decomposition of it.

\paragraph{The agreed direction.} The panel runs two arms, both cooled quasi-statically: the crucible,
and the same cool on the whole length distribution from the first epoch, so a failure in the
crucible arm alone names the crucible's harm on a backdrop (adaptive cooling waits as a refinement:
no shape, octave or cool-length constants). Runs stop on their answers alone, at the first step
whose snapped machine answers right within a state bound; the certificate only judges the stop.
Once the first option is decided, the literature's other options
for formation and conversion (noise in the cell \citep{oliva-2020-stability-internal-states-rnn-regular-languages},
the rotation trick \citep{fifty-2024-rotation-trick-vector-quantization}, fixed-grid factors
\citep{mentzer-2023-finite-scalar-quantization}) are shelved rather than tried in turn. The method is
run on the full panel of backdrops (the $S_3$, $S_4$ and $S_5$ word problems, move-to-front on 4 and
5 items, modular division at 169 states, the runs that needed the commitment term, and fresh $S_3$
seeds) to find the failing cases. Those are analysed for a common mechanism, each failure classified
as formation (continuous separation), conversion (snapped against continuous) or extra codes (codes
standing for several states: the likely kind, but not presumed). A
remedy is then chosen by its fit to that mechanism. Each shelved option is recorded with the wall it
fits (noise: thin margins; the rotation trick: windows of different states sharing a code; fixed
grids: a collapsing codebook), so the diagnosis can call one down.

\section{What is not established}
\label{sec:open}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{Generality and harm}: the panel hasn't run; the harm runs certified before only with
  the commitment term on.
  \item \emph{Rates}: one cell a backdrop; $p$ on fresh seeds, and on harder backdrops where it can
  discriminate, is unmeasured. Adoption as the general method needs six seeds a backdrop.
  \item \emph{Two registered probes still running}: the handover probe's $S_3$ arm (expected to fail:
  no curriculum on a group), and adaptive cooling's existence probe ($S_3$ seeds 0--7 and
  move-to-front seed 3; its bar at least 4 of 8 $S_3$ certificates). \emph{Read in flight, not a
  verdict}: seeds 0, 2 and 4 certified (11.5, 2.4 and 21.5 minutes; the quasi-static crucible's 4--28),
  while seeds 1, 3 and 5, which the quasi-static crucible certified, formed the state (best shares
  0.73--0.99) and lost it. Below the cascade the capacity falls to about 0.01 while the coupling is
  still about half way, so the steps grow to the octave cap and the coupling, which follows the
  temperature, rises up to 0.65 in an epoch (the quasi-static cool's never above 0.02). Each jump drops
  the screen and the clock holds, the network recovers, the next jump drops it again, until the state
  erodes. A bound on the step (the coupling's rise at most 0.02 a step) is built and registered as
  its own probe.
  \item \emph{Move-to-front in the crucible}: seed 3 hasn't certified in the crucible under either
  cool (best state shares about 0.93); it certified without one.
  \item \emph{Extra codes} (codes whose outputs go wrong within a few tokens, the random machines'
  failure) is untouched.
  \item \emph{Discovery proper} still needs a state bound, and the certificate needs the target. Every
  rate above is a known-target rate: each run stopped at its first certificate. Re-read offline, a
  stop on the answers alone with the bound at the target's size kept every certificate, but a bound
  10\% slack stopped 25 runs on uncertified networks, one a false discovery; the panel stops on the answers, at the exact bound.
\end{itemize}
